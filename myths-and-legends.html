Myths & Legends of the Deep

Long before science could explain the ocean's depths, sailors told stories of what lurked below. Some of those stories turned out to be closer to the truth than anyone expected.

The Kraken

Norse sailors told tales of the Kraken, a monster so massive it could drag entire ships beneath the waves. Historians now believe the legend grew from real sightings of giant squid washing ashore or surfacing near boats, a creature large and strange enough to be mistaken for a sea monster.

Mermaids & Sirens

Stories of mermaids appear in cultures across the world, from Greek sirens to Irish selkies. Many historians point to sailors mistaking manatees and dugongs, seen from a distance after months at sea, as the likely origin of these enduring legends.

The Bloop

In 1997, hydrophones thousands of miles apart picked up an enormously loud, low-frequency sound from the Pacific. Nicknamed "The Bloop," it sparked years of speculation about an undiscovered giant sea creature before researchers concluded it most likely came from a large icequake in Antarctica.

Vanishing Vessels

Throughout history, ships and submarines have vanished without a trace, feeding legends of ghost vessels and cursed waters. While some disappearances remain genuinely unexplained, most are now attributed to storms, equipment failure, or navigational error rather than anything supernatural.
