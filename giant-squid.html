🦑 Giant Squid

The elusive giant of the deep that inspired centuries of sea monster legends.

Architeuthis dux

The giant squid can grow up to 13 metres long, making it one of the largest invertebrates on Earth. It lives in the deep ocean, usually between 300 and 1,000 metres down, which is part of why it stayed a mystery for so long. It wasn't photographed alive in its natural habitat until 2004, and the first video footage of one swimming freely wasn't captured until 2012.

Giant squid have the largest eyes of any animal alive, up to 27 centimetres across, roughly the size of a dinner plate. Scientists believe these enormous eyes help them detect the faint bioluminescent glow of predators like sperm whales in the pitch black depths.

Circular sucker scars found on the skin of sperm whales are some of the best evidence of battles between the two giants, since a living giant squid in the act of fighting one has still never been documented by humans.

Sightings and remains of giant squid washing ashore over hundreds of years are widely believed to be the real origin of the Kraken legend. Read more about that connection on the Myths & Legends page.
